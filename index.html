Services

Five ways I can help an upstream team

Each piece of work is built for your instruments, your file formats and the people who will use the output.

Instrument data pipelines

Scripts that collect exports from your bioreactors, cell counters and analysers, check them for gaps and errors, and combine them into one tidy dataset per experiment. Outputs include summary Excel files, standard plots and files ready for JMP.

BioreactorsAutomated cell countersMetabolite analysersOffline samples

Dashboards and self-serve data

Dashboards that let scientists and managers compare runs, clones and conditions themselves. Where your platform supports it, I can set up plain-English querying so people can ask questions of the data directly.

DatabricksR ShinyPython and Plotly

Seed train and process planning

Planning tools that work out passage volumes, split ratios and timings from shake flask through rocking bioreactors to the production vessel, so teams spend less time on spreadsheets before every run.

Shake flasksRocking bioreactorsN-1Bench to pilot scale

Training and adoption

A new tool only helps if people use it. I run hands-on sessions for scientists and managers, write short guides in plain language, and hand over everything so your team can maintain it.

WorkshopsUser guidesHandover documentation

Pattern finding and machine learning

Group runs, clones and conditions that behave alike, flag runs that don't fit the pattern, and find which process settings best separate good outcomes from poor ones. I use established methods, check how well each model holds up, and report the results in plain language with the plots that support them.

About

Bench scientist first, data person second

I worked in cell culture development at CSL for 20 years, mostly in upstream R&D with CHO cells producing monoclonal antibodies and recombinant coagulation factors. I prepared and ran seed trains, high-throughput micro-bioreactor studies and rocking bioreactor expansions, and supported scale-up to pilot and manufacturing.

In my later years there I wrote the processing scripts for our micro-bioreactor data. A data engineer then built them into a Databricks pipeline that processed 50 to 70 experiments. I also introduced a natural-language AI assistant to the department and trained scientists and managers to query their data in plain English.

I now work independently, helping smaller teams get the same value from their data without needing an in-house data group.
